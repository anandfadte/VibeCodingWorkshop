# Brief
> Understand the scenario. 5 minutes. Know the business before you build anything.

## scenario

**Client:** Meridian Retail Group
**Your role:** Genpact Account Team — you manage finance reporting and analytics operations for Meridian. This is their quarterly performance review with their own leadership.
**What you're building:** Single-file interactive HTML QBR dashboard
**Who will read it:** Leadership team — quarterly business review, tomorrow morning
**Files in your folder:** `meridian_q4_data.xlsx` · `genpact_logo.png` · `screenshot_1.png` · `screenshot_2.png`

The screenshots set the **design standard** — layout, colors, component style. Any numbers visible in the screenshots are illustrative. The actual data comes from the Excel file.

The last QBR deck landed flat. The CFO asked three questions about at-risk clients and Q4 trajectory that the static slides couldn't answer in the room. The meeting ran over, the action list was vague, and the follow-up email took three days. This time you have the data, the design references, and 40 minutes. Build something they can interact with — so the conversation moves forward, not sideways.

## do_this

1. Open `meridian_q4_data.xlsx` — look at Revenue Summary and Client Risk Table. 3 minutes.
2. Open `screenshot_1.png` and `screenshot_2.png` — note the layout and visual style. 1 minute.
3. When you can describe the quarter's story and name the riskiest client from memory, move to Frame.
