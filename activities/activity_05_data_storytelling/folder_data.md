# Build & Iterate
> Fire. Test. Improve once. Ship.

## scenario

Open your AI tool. Attach all five files. Paste your prompt. You have 20 minutes — 10 for the first build, 10 for one targeted improvement.

## do_this

**Round 1 — Build (10 minutes):**
1. Open Claude / GitHub Copilot (or your AI tool) in a new tab
2. Attach `campaign_q3_results.xlsx`, `q4_budget_brief.pdf`, `screenshot_1.png`, `screenshot_2.png`, and `genpact_logo.png`
3. Paste your completed prompt
4. Copy the full HTML code block from the response
5. Save as `eis_campaign_report.html` and open in Chrome or Edge

**Check these three things before Round 2:**
- Does the headline miss land first? Verify the opportunity attainment % against the Campaign Summary sheet in the Excel file
- Is the channel comparison the central visual — the section the eye goes to after the headline?
- Does the report have a clear Q4 recommendation section, not just charts?

If the page doesn't open or shows errors, check the browser console (F12 → Console). A common fix: paste the HTML back to your AI and say "fix any JavaScript errors — the page is not rendering."

**Round 2 — Improve once (10 minutes):**
6. Identify the single most important gap between your output and what you aimed for in Frame
7. Write one focused follow-up: "Change only [specific element] to [specific improvement]"
8. Fire it in the same conversation — do not start a new chat
9. Copy the new HTML and test again

The constraint matters: one gap, one fix. This builds the habit of targeted iteration over re-prompting from scratch.

## checkpoint

Before presenting:
- The headline miss is the visual opening — opportunity attainment lands with impact
- The channel CPO comparison makes the best and worst performers unmistakably clear
- At least 2–3 figures are verified against `campaign_q3_results.xlsx`
- You can describe in one sentence what your Round 2 improvement changed and why

## reveal_good

What separates a campaign report that changes budget decisions from one that gets acknowledged and forgotten is the difference between a data summary and a data story.

A data summary shows what happened. A data story shows what it means and what must change. The channel CPO comparison is data. "Executive Roundtables deliver 4× more opportunities per dollar than Trade Shows — shift budget now" is a story.

The strongest first builds in this exercise have a recommendation section, not just charts. If your first build ends with a chart, Round 2 is about adding the conclusion.

## reveal_takeaway

Every participant built from the same campaign data. The report you built reflects your verdict about what Q4 should look like — which channels to back, which to cut, what the room needs to decide.

That is what professional vibe coding looks like: the AI executes, you direct. The judgment about what the data means and what should change always belongs to you.
