# Build & Iterate
> Fire. Test. Improve once. Ship.

## scenario

Open your AI tool. Attach all five files. Paste your prompt. You have 20 minutes — 10 for the first build, 10 for one targeted improvement.

## do_this

**Round 1 — Build (10 minutes):**
1. Open Claude / GitHub Copilot (or your AI tool) in a new tab
2. Attach `calloway_deal_data.xlsx`, `calloway_competitor_grid.pdf`, `screenshot_1.png`, `screenshot_2.png`, and `genpact_logo.png`
3. Paste your completed prompt
4. Copy the full HTML code block from the response
5. Save as `calloway_microsite.html` and open in Chrome or Edge

**Check these three things before Round 2:**
- Does the ROI calculation look right? Verify the 3-year benefit and ROI % against the ROI Model sheet in the Excel file
- Is the hero section — the ROI case — the most prominent thing above the fold?
- Does Robert Chen's specific concern appear in the stakeholder or objection section?

If the page doesn't open or shows errors, check the browser console (F12 → Console). A common fix: paste the HTML back to your AI and say "fix any JavaScript errors — the page is not rendering."

**Round 2 — Improve once (10 minutes):**
6. Identify the single most important gap between your output and the deal you are trying to close
7. Write one focused follow-up: "Change only [specific element] to [specific improvement]"
8. Fire it in the same conversation — do not start a new chat
9. Copy the new HTML and test again

The constraint matters: one gap, one fix. This builds the habit of targeted iteration over re-prompting from scratch.

## checkpoint

Before presenting:
- The ROI hero section is visually dominant — three numbers, prominent above the fold
- Robert Chen's objection and the specific proof point addressing it are both visible
- At least 2–3 figures are verified against `calloway_deal_data.xlsx`
- You can describe in one sentence what your Round 2 improvement changed and why

## reveal_good

What separates a proposal microsite that closes a deal from one that gets ignored is not the design template. It is whether the page opens with the buyer's problem — not Genpact's capabilities.

The strongest first builds in this exercise open with a quantified cost of inaction, not "Genpact's approach to transformation." The weakest open with a company overview.

If you have Robert Chen's specific past experience visible on page load, you have already done more personalisation than most proposals ever manage.

## reveal_takeaway

Every participant built from the same deal data. The microsite you built reflects your read of what Robert Chen needs to see to say yes — the opening hook, the proof point choice, the CTA framing.

That is what professional vibe coding looks like: the AI executes, you direct. Judgment — about the buyer, the objection, the close — always belongs to you.
